On the sleep row specifically. The ~6 h figure is what the watch recorded on the nights it was worn — and it isn't worn every night. That makes it a sample, not an average, and the bias could run in either direction: if the watch comes off on nights you crash early, the number is too low; if it's mostly worn on ordinary home nights, it may miss your worst ones. There is no way to tell from the data which is happening, so this card no longer treats 6 h as established, and the tag has been downgraded from limiting to unreliable sample. Fixing it is cheap: wear it five to seven nights in a row, once, covering both shift and off nights. That single stretch is worth more than months of sporadic wear, and it settles a row that quietly gates every other row in this table.

Three of these are assets. The three still marked limiting are levers — and all three move with the same inputs: training volume, protein, and sleep. The two orthopedic rows are different: they're unknowns, not limits, and one imaging appointment converts them into either an asset or a plan. Sleep now sits in that same unknown category, which is the more useful place for it: an open question you can close this week rather than a verdict you're stuck with.

what to send · what it changes

This card is a living index, not a snapshot. Every rating carries a confidence tag, and every tag has a specific input that upgrades it. Send data in chat — it gets logged to Training Log.md, the ratings recompute, and this same URL updates. Nothing here is fixed.

A timed run, row, or benchmark WOD. Apple Health workout summaries work directly. Replaces the inferred Stamina and Speed marks.

An overhead squat or sit-and-reach photo scores Flexibility for real. A wall-ball or target set scores Accuracy. Both currently run on inference alone.

Health data

Labs, weight, sleep, BP, body-comp scans. These set the caps in the panel above — new bloodwork can raise a ceiling, not just a rating.

Useful cadence: weight weekly, training weekly, a lift or conditioning benchmark monthly, labs quarterly. Anything more frequent measures noise.

How these numbers are built. Ratings are anchored to measured data where it exists — VO₂ max 35.8 and a logged 225 lb squat session are real, as are repeated clinical findings of normal range of motion, gait, and coordination. Where no measurement exists, ratings are inferred from body composition, training history, occupational demand, and documented health markers, and are tagged accordingly in the Prediction Index. Flexibility is estimated from body-fat-driven mechanical restriction, prior foot surgery, and the absence of any mobility training, offset by consistently normal clinical ROM. Accuracy is estimated from occupational precision demands, documented technical lifting, and normal coordination exams, offset by ADHD-related motor-timing variability and chronic short sleep — both of which are modifiable. The ceiling is a forward projection of realistic trained capacity at goal bodyweight for a 32-year-old male of this frame; it is not a historical figure. Projections assume ~1.0–1.5 lb/week loss with resistance training and adequate protein. These are estimates under consistency, not guarantees — and every one of them is designed to be overwritten by a real number.
