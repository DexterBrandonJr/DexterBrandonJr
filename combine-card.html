What Is Actually Driving The Loss

Sep 1 — what this data can and cannot separate

Six inputs started at roughly the same time: tirzepatide (really 5 mg), paradoxine, the supplement stack, high-protein eating, CrossFit volume, and consistency. One weight series cannot separate six simultaneous interventions — that is a limit of the study design, not of the arithmetic. What the series can do is bound how large an effect would have to be before it became visible at all.

The detection floor is the number that settles this. With 16 clean readings over 39 days and a residual scatter of about 1.1 lb, this series resolves a difference of roughly 0.57 lb/week — about 284 kcal/day. Paradoxine's published effect and the daily-averaged cost of six CrossFit sessions a month both land under that. So neither would show up as its own line in the data even with perfect logging, and any confident claim that paradoxine is or isn't working would be invented, not measured.

What the bundle is doing, measured as a bundle: 5.8% of starting body weight in 5.6 weeks. Published 5 mg trial trajectories are near 4.5% at that point — so the whole stack together is running about 1.3× population pace. That margin is the honest home for training, protein and consistency: it is real, it is measurable, and it is not attributable to any one of them individually.

The one place diet shows up directly is lean mass, and there the news is flat. Lean share of loss is 24.8% over the whole window and 24.9% over the second half — no improvement. If the ~185–200 g/day protein target were being hit consistently, this is the number that should be bending downward. It isn't. That is the single strongest argument for logging intake rather than aiming at it.

To make any of this separable would take one input changing while the others hold, or direct measurement — daily active-energy burn, logged protein grams, a step count. None exist in the record today, and adding a step count is the cheapest of the three.

Feeding The Tracker

what to send · what it changes

This card is a living index, not a snapshot. Every rating carries a confidence tag, and every tag has a specific input that upgrades it. Send data in chat — it gets logged to Training Log.md, the ratings recompute, and this same URL updates. Nothing here is fixed.

Lifts

Any 1RM/3RM — bench, squat, deadlift, press. Moves Strength, Power, and the confidence score in one shot. Bench is the single largest blind spot.

Conditioning

A timed run, row, or benchmark WOD. Apple Health workout summaries work directly. Replaces the inferred Stamina and Speed marks.

Mobility & control

An overhead squat or sit-and-reach photo scores Flexibility for real. A wall-ball or target set scores Accuracy. Both currently run on inference alone.

Health data

Labs, weight, sleep, BP, body-comp scans. These set the caps in the panel above — new bloodwork can raise a ceiling, not just a rating.

Useful cadence: weight weekly, training weekly, a lift or conditioning benchmark monthly, labs quarterly. Anything more frequent measures noise.

How these numbers are built. Ratings are anchored to measured data where it exists — VO₂ max 39–40.6 (measured Aug 8) and the Aug 14 true 1RMs (squat 385, deadlift 405, strict press 165) are real, as are repeated clinical findings of normal range of motion, gait, and coordination. Where no measurement exists, ratings are inferred from body composition, training history, occupational demand, and documented health markers, and are tagged accordingly in the Prediction Index. Flexibility is estimated from body-fat-driven mechanical restriction, prior foot surgery, and the absence of any mobility training, offset by consistently normal clinical ROM. Accuracy is estimated from occupational precision demands, documented technical lifting, and normal coordination exams, offset by ADHD-related motor-timing variability and confirmed short sleep — the second of which is modifiable. The ceiling is a forward projection of realistic trained capacity at goal bodyweight for a 32-year-old male of this frame; it is not a historical figure. Projections are anchored to the measured −2.44 lb/week with deceleration applied, not to a fixed linear assumption. These are estimates under consistency, not guarantees — and every one of them is designed to be overwritten by a real number.
