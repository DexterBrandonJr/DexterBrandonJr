## 2026-09-07 — QA/QC pass: OTOCO brackets solve device-independence; flagged a real position-size gap

**Decisions:** Every approved trade will be submitted as a single broker-side OTOCO bracket order (entry + take-profit + stop-loss together), not a bare entry -- research confirmed broker-managed conditional orders execute on price once placed, independent of any live connection to our code, Dex's phone, or his laptop. That reframes what "works even if my phone is off" actually needs: it's an order-type choice available from trade #1, not something that has to wait for a later, separately-earned autonomy stage. Stage 2 was renamed/narrowed accordingly, from "the bot places exit orders" (brackets already do that structurally) to "the bot may autonomously adjust a working stop" -- a real, still-gated code action. The gate's size cap is now a config value rather than a hardcoded number, defaulting conservative until Dex confirms it.
**Facts / preferences:** Real tension surfaced between Dex's own written 10-15% single-trade allocation rule and standard options risk-of-ruin math -- at 10-15% risk per trade, a 6-9 trade losing streak halves an account, and the real trade sample already shows a sub-50% win rate where that streak length is an expected event, not a tail risk. Flagged to Dex directly rather than silently changed; his account sizes are small enough that a flat 1% rule (the usual professional floor) isn't practical either, so the real lever recommended is strategy selection -- defined-risk verticals/spreads over naked long calls -- rather than percentage math alone. Two more free signal sources identified for setup detection: CBOE's free delayed-quote API for unusual options volume-vs-open-interest activity, and free gamma-exposure/dealer-positioning data as a market-regime signal (positive GEX favors premium-selling, negative GEX favors long-premium directional plays).

## 2026-09-07 — Final QA/QC round 2: decided 5% size cap, added circuit breaker + signal decay self-assessment

**Decisions:** Dex delegated the size-cap decision explicitly ("you decide"). Settled on 5% of total combined liquidity per single trade, hard ceiling, down from the written 10-15% -- chosen because it lines up exactly with the existing 20% cross-account correlation ceiling (four 5% positions = that ceiling) and leaves real margin against the measured sub-50% win rate, while staying practical at these account sizes (unlike the textbook 1% floor). A one-contract floor exception applies when 5% computes below the cheapest qualifying defined-risk structure's cost, flagged distinctly rather than blended into normal sizing. Dex also asked to build toward the standing goal already on record elsewhere in this repo -- an autonomous system that self-corrects, self-develops, and is self-aware of its own function -- applied concretely here rather than left abstract: self-correction is a circuit breaker (drawdown/consecutive-loss/daily-loss triggers, any one of which reverts every account to manual-only and alerts Dex) that is structurally as immutable as the risk-rules file itself; self-awareness is a rolling per-signal-source scorecard that automatically down-weights (never silently drops) a source whose linked trades decay to breakeven or worse; self-development extends the existing autopatch pattern with a labeled strategy-tuning PR category -- evidence-backed proposals only, never an autonomous change to the risk rules or the circuit breaker itself.
**Facts / preferences:** Broker-side conditional orders (confirmed for Webull specifically: OTO/OCO/OTOCO combo orders are supported) execute on price once placed, independent of any live connection -- this is the actual mechanism satisfying "works even if my phone or laptop is off," not a feature of any autonomy stage. Real risk-management pattern confirmed across retail and institutional sources: layered circuit breakers (drawdown tighter than backtested worst-case, consecutive-loss counters, daily loss limits) rather than relying on any single trigger, and signal/alpha decay is a recognized, monitorable phenomenon (rolling performance windows, not a one-time backtest) -- both patterns adopted directly rather than invented from scratch.
